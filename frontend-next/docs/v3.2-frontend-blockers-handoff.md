## B1 — closure is separate from verified resolution

The candidate used `!verifiedResolution(c)` as the open-case predicate. A resolved queue row has no outcome detail yet, so it entered the active table, open-case count and Waiting rail. The service also permanently sliced the resolved enrichment population to 30 records.

`caseQueueGroup` now distinguishes active, reported-resolved/unconfirmed, and verified-resolved cases. Reported closure never enters the active table, open-case count or Waiting tab. It is accessible in Resolved with a neutral clock and an explicit verification-pending/unavailable label. Only a fresh loaded detail, resolved workflow and authoritative V3.2 resolution flag (or the existing legacy verified-outcome fallback) allow a green verified check. Previous-cycle or invalidated details cannot supply confirmation after a workflow/version change. The rail separately counts unconfirmed rows and verified outcomes.

Resolved enrichment rotates eight records per poll across the entire loaded resolved queue, instead of restricting eligibility to the first 30. Verified details are skipped; unconfirmed details, including HTTP-success payloads without verification, remain eligible for later attempts. Existing queue pagination bounds, the 24-record total poll-enrichment cap, three-request poll pool, selected-case priority, in-flight deduplication and stale-response guards remain. Selected watches/SSE reads can still add deduplicated reads outside that pool, as in the candidate.

The regression uses 45 resolved rows paged in groups of 15, holds three detail reads, exercises 404/503 failures and a successful detail without verifier evidence, and checks repeated polling, later recovery and the concurrency bound. A browser check verifies that all 45 stay out of active/Waiting, missing confirmation never produces green checks, and only the authoritative detail creates one verified entry.

The 45-row capture also exposed the rail's flex growth overriding its intended scroll height. Its existing scroll region now keeps its fixed height, with the final row reachable by scrolling, instead of stretching the entire operations page to fit every entry.
